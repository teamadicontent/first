# Signal Outreach Craft

How to audit a prospect's LinkedIn and write cold outreach that gets replies. The standard: if the prospect would screenshot the message and post it as an example of AI spam, it failed. If it reads like a busy, sharp operator typed it in 40 seconds because they genuinely noticed something, it passed.

## Part 1 — The audit (do this before writing one word)

The audit is not a formality; it produces the raw material every message is built from. Spend the research budget here.

Look at the prospect's last 10–15 LinkedIn posts (or note their absence) and answer:

1. **Frequency and recency.** Silent for months? Sporadic bursts? Posting weekly? The gap IS the pitch for silent founders; wasted effort is the pitch for frequent-but-flat posters.
2. **The substitution test** (the house core filter): could this exact post have been published by any founder in their industry? If yes, it's failing them. Note the most generic recent post specifically.
3. **Voice check.** First person and opinionated, or press-release third person? Company updates dressed as thought leadership?
4. **The buried asset.** What does this person uniquely know that they are NOT writing about? A founder who scaled to 1,000 restaurant locations posting product screenshots is sitting on stories nobody else can tell. Name the asset.
5. **Engagement vs. audience.** 15k followers but 8 likes per post = audience exists, content isn't activating it. Their funding announcement got 400 reactions but their next post got 12 = attention decays without a system.

Output: 2–3 audit observations, each specific enough that ONLY someone who actually looked could have written it. "Your content could be stronger" is not an observation. "Your last six posts are product updates, but the one where you told the story about your first restaurant customer outperformed all of them combined" is.

## Part 2 — Writing rules (hard constraints)

### Banned outright
- Em dashes and en dashes. Use commas, periods, or restructure.
- AI vocabulary: delve, leverage (as a verb), unlock, elevate, supercharge, seamless, game-changer, landscape, realm, robust, streamline, harness, empower, journey, resonate, dive deep, navigate (metaphorically), tapestry, testament.
- AI openers: "I hope this finds you well", "I came across your profile", "I couldn't help but notice", "I was impressed by", "As a [role] myself".
- AI structures: "Not just X, but Y". "X isn't about Y. It's about Z." Rule-of-three adjective stacks ("simple, effective, and powerful"). Rhetorical questions as openers ("Ever wondered why...?").
- Emojis, bold text, bullet points inside messages. A cold message is a note, not a deck.
- Fake familiarity: "loved your recent post" without naming the post. "Big fan of what you're building" without evidence.
- Flattery ramps: two sentences of praise before the point. One specific observation beats five compliments.

### Required
- Lead with the specific observation, never with who you are. The first line must prove you looked.
- One idea per message. If the DM makes two points, cut one.
- Give first: every message contains something the prospect can use even if they never reply (an observation about their content, a concrete post angle, a pattern from their space).
- Soft close. "Happy to share what's working, no pitch" / "Thought you'd find this useful" / "Worth a look sometime?" Never "Can we hop on a call" or calendar links in a first message.
- Simple words. Short sentences mixed with a longer one. Read it aloud; if any sentence needs a breath in the middle, split it.
- Sound like the house voice: direct, a little assertive, zero corporate padding. Someone who has done this for dozens of founders and knows it, without saying it.

### Credibility, dosed
One line maximum, only in DMs/emails, never in the connection invite beyond a parenthetical. "I ghostwrite for SaaS founders (Peak XV, Tibo)" is complete. Listing metrics (250M impressions, 40 clients, six countries) in a first message reads as a pitch deck; save it for when they ask.

## Part 3 — The three message types

### Connection invite (≤300 characters, hard limit, count every time)
Structure: specific observation → one clause of relevance → soft close. No pitch. State the character count under every draft; if over 300, cut and recount.

### LinkedIn DM (post-acceptance, 60–120 words)
Structure: (1) the audit observation, delivered plainly, 1–2 lines. (2) One concrete, usable idea: a post angle from their buried asset, a fix for their weakest pattern. (3) Soft door.

### Cold email (80–130 words, only with a verified address)
Subject: 3–6 words, specific, lowercase-casual works ("your linkedin after the raise", "one post idea for allO"). Body: same give-first structure as the DM plus the one credibility line. Hardest close allowed: "worth a 15-min look sometime?"

## Part 4 — Calibration examples

### Bad invite (what AI writes)
> Hi Cancan! I hope this message finds you well. I came across your profile and was truly impressed by allO's incredible journey in revolutionizing the restaurant tech landscape. I'd love to connect and explore potential synergies!

Why it fails: zero evidence of looking, three banned phrases, "synergies", exclamation marks doing the work specificity should.

### Good invite (283 chars)
> Congrats on the Zigg round. Noticed something: your funding post pulled 400+ reactions but the product posts since barely move. That attention has a half-life of weeks. I ghostwrite for SaaS founders (Peak XV, Tibo). Happy to share what usually fixes this, no pitch.

Why it works: proves the look in line one, names a real pattern, gives a mini-insight (attention decay) for free, credibility in one parenthetical, soft close.

### Bad DM
> Thanks for connecting! I wanted to reach out because I help founders like you elevate their personal brand and unlock the power of LinkedIn to drive meaningful engagement. Would you be open to a quick 15-minute call to explore how we could work together?

### Good DM
> Looked through your last couple months of posts before sending that invite. The pattern: company updates in company voice. The post that broke out was the one where you wrote about your first restaurant customer in Munich, because nobody else can post that story. That's the whole unlock for founder content. You have maybe 20 of those stories from scaling to 1,000 locations. If you wrote one a week in first person, your reach problem disappears. Thought you'd find that useful either way.

Why it works: names their actual best post, diagnoses the pattern, hands them a usable content strategy whether or not they reply, no ask at all.

### Bad email subject → good subject
"Elevate Your LinkedIn Presence with Proven Ghostwriting" → "your linkedin after the raise"

## Part 5 — Final check before delivering any message

Read each draft and ask: Does line one prove I looked? Is there anything they can use without replying? Would this survive being read aloud in a normal speaking voice? Zero banned words, zero em dashes? Invite ≤300 characters, counted? If any answer is no, rewrite before delivering. Never deliver a message you would not send from your own account.
