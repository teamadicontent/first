# Loops

A loop is any promise the writer makes that the reader expects to be paid: a question, a "something", a number teased, a story started, a "here's why". Loops are how posts hold attention. They are also how posts lose trust, because an unpaid loop is a broken promise, and a reader who has been handed two promises by a stranger in the first two lines starts reading to check rather than to learn.

## The trust-budget model

The original question was "open a new loop before closing the previous one, or close loops quickly and risk losing curiosity?" That is the wrong binary. The right rule:

**A loop only works if the reader already cares about the answer.** Caring is bought with trust, and trust is paid for by closing a previous loop well or by a specific that only this author could have.

So:

- **Stacking** (opening loop 2 before closing loop 1) is fine when loop 1 has already bought credit. A dated story with a place and a named person buys credit on its own, even before it closes, because the reader trusts that a real scene will pay off. An abstraction ("I've realized something") buys nothing.
- **Sequencing** (close loop 1, then open loop 2) is the safe default for commentary posts, framework posts, and anything whose first loop is an idea rather than a scene.
- **Closing fast** does not lose curiosity if the close itself opens the next question. The product ladder in the Stripe example closes "why Stripe?" and in doing so raises "so what does OpenRouter remove?" That is sequencing that feels like stacking.

## Types of loop and what pays them

| Loop type | Opened by | Paid by |
|---|---|---|
| Question | "Why was it Stripe?" | A direct answer, then evidence |
| Tease | "I've realized something" | The specific thing, within two lines |
| Number promise | "one chart tells you" | The chart's number, with unit and period |
| Story | "In 2011, in a Bangalore apartment..." | The turn (what changed) |
| Framework | "There are three things here" | All three, in the announced order |
| Contrast | "Everyone thinks X" | Y, with a reason X is wrong |

A loop is paid when the reader could state the answer in one sentence. "It's about mission" pays "why Stripe?" only if the next lines make "mission" concrete; otherwise it is a loop answered with another loop.

## Loop debt

Count the loops open at each line. The reader's tolerance:

- One open loop: fine indefinitely, if the post is moving.
- Two open loops: fine for a few lines if the first is a scene; fatal if both are abstractions.
- Three open loops in the first three lines: the reader is holding IOUs from a stranger. Readability and trust both drop.

An unpaid loop at the end of the post is worse than no loop. The reader remembers the promise, not the rest.

## Writing the loop audit

Format in the editorial:

```
**Loop audit**

- Opened: "quoted phrase" (L1), "quoted phrase" (L2), ...
- Paid: which ones, and where (L-number).
- Call: sequential or stacked for this post, with the one-sentence reason.
```

Rules:

- Quote the opening phrase, with line marker. Do not describe it.
- "Paid" means the reader could state the answer. Late and weak payoffs count as paid but get noted.
- The call is specific to this post and this author. "Stacked loops work for Vivek when the first is a dated story; they don't work on abstractions" is a call. "Consider your loop structure" is not.
- If the post has no loops, say so, and say whether it needs one. Some purest-tier posts for a peer audience are better with a flat opening and no tease at all; the specificity is the hook.

## Author-specific loop habits

Check the voice skill for these before judging. Known patterns:

- **Vivek**: announces structure ("I'll answer this in three frames") which is a framework loop that pre-commits to paying in order. Opens in the middle of a story or on a number. States both branches before his position, which is a contrast loop that closes on the second branch. Never leaves a loop open for engagement's sake.
- **Aditya (`aditya-voice`)**: verdict-first. His strongest posts open on the result and the loop is "how?", paid by the examples. He should almost never open with a tease.

## The one structural question to answer every time

Where is the post's spine (its best section), and how many lines does the reader wait before reaching it? If the answer is more than two, the loops before the spine are costing more than they earn. Move the spine up or cut what precedes it.
