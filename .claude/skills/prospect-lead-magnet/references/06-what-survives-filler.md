# What survives filler removal

A tell-checker catches vocabulary ("delve", "tapestry"), formatting (bold-heavy, triadic lists), and known fingerprints. A draft can be clean on all of that and still read as written by nobody in particular. These are the patterns that get through. Each has a mechanism (why it reads as artificial), a cost, and the usual fix. Use the mechanism name in the Fault line of the editorial; it transfers to the next post.

## Opening faults

**Credential stapled to a drumroll**
- Shape: "After N years doing X, I've realized something:" / "Here's what nobody tells you about Y:"
- Mechanism: the first line is about the author and a promise, not the idea. The reader is asked to extend credit before anything is delivered.
- Cost: the target reader (a peer, not a fan) skims past in the first 1.5 seconds.
- Fix: cut, open on the thing itself (the deal, the number, the scene).

**Corrective opener**
- Shape: "While everyone is obsessing over X, the real question is Y." / "And no, it's not just Z."
- Mechanism: positions the author against an imagined crowd. Reads as pundit, not peer. Vivek flagged this pattern by name.
- Cost: the reader is told what to think about other people's thinking before they have been given a thought.
- Fix: state X flatly, then state Y. Drop the crowd.

**Promise without delivery window**
- Shape: "A lot of decisions can be made by looking at a single graph." (then no graph for nine lines)
- Mechanism: a loop is opened with no signal of when it closes.
- Cost: an IOU the reader carries; if they forget it, the eventual payoff lands flat.
- Fix: either deliver the thing in the next line or cut the promise.

## Middle faults

**Pre-announcing the evidence**
- Shape: "And everything they do is in line with that." followed by four lines that prove it.
- Mechanism: the sentence summarises what is about to be shown. It exists only to reach the next sentence.
- Cost: pure filler wearing a claim's clothes; the proof then lands as confirmation instead of discovery.
- Fix: cut.

**Restating the list**
- Shape: a list of three specifics, then "Everything they do is to remove X."
- Mechanism: the reader just watched the pattern; naming it again spends the payoff twice.
- Cost: the best section in the post loses its punch on the line after it.
- Fix: cut, or compress to a five-word tag that adds a new angle.

**Doing the reader's thinking for them**
- Shape: "X fits that template." / "This is exactly what I mean." / "Which brings me to..."
- Mechanism: a bridge that tells the reader what conclusion to draw instead of placing the next fact so the conclusion is unavoidable.
- Cost: condescends to the target reader; weakens the bridge into the next stat.
- Fix: put the next fact in the same grammar as the preceding pattern so it slots in without commentary.

**Grammar drift inside a list**
- Shape: "Payments - getting money was hard / Atlas, because starting was hard / Then Issuing, Capital, Tax."
- Mechanism: three items, three sentence shapes. The third drops the pattern that made the first two land.
- Cost: the one section that works loses its rhythm on its last beat.
- Fix: one grammar, repeated, and the last item should be the longest or the hardest hit.

**One-word paragraph carrying an abstraction**
- Shape: "Mission." / "Focus." / "Trust." as a standalone line.
- Mechanism: one-word lines work when the word is concrete or a punchline ("We made 4,000 rupees."). On an abstraction they read like a slide title.
- Cost: feels staged; breaks the author's announce-then-deliver habit if they have one.
- Fix: "One-word answer: mission. Longer answer below." or fold the word into the next sentence.

**Claim-shaped sentence with no specific inside it**
- Shape: "A lot of business decisions..." / "Most founders..." / "The best teams..."
- Mechanism: quantifier plus category plus verdict, with no number, name, date, or scene.
- Cost: fails the author's own kill filter if they have one (Vivek: every claim gets a number or a story). Fails the substitution test always.
- Fix: replace with the specific, or cut.

**Rhetorical question answered by the author**
- Shape: "How long before we cross X? I don't think long."
- Mechanism: simulates dialogue to smuggle in a stance. On LinkedIn it also reads as an engagement bait pattern.
- Cost: if the stance is not one the author has stated, it violates the attribution rule. If it is, the question was padding.
- Fix: state the stance flatly with its probability, or cut the forward claim.

## Closing faults

**Writer's flourish in the last line**
- Shape: "...measured in the unit it now moves in." / "...and that changes everything."
- Mechanism: the sentence is shaped for the writer's satisfaction, not the reader's. Most founders close flat; a ghostwriter closes pretty.
- Cost: the last line is the one the reader remembers, and it is the one that sounds ghostwritten.
- Fix: the author's close. Short, declarative, often self-deprecating or a concrete peer question.

**Engagement question bolted on**
- Shape: "What do you think?" / "Agree?"
- Mechanism: asks for a reply without having earned one.
- Cost: flagged tell for Vivek specifically; reads as bait to any peer audience.
- Fix: cut, or replace with a question only a peer could answer ("How are your debriefs structured?").

**Close that restates the hook**
- Mechanism: loops back to line one as if symmetry were a payoff.
- Cost: the post ends where it started; the reader gains nothing from the last line.
- Fix: close on the spine's energy, one step further than the hook.

## Whole-post faults

**Commentary wearing the author's name**
- Mechanism: a take on public news with no story, number, or experience from the author's own life or company.
- Cost: fails the substitution test by construction. Any commentator could post it.
- Fix: one line that only this author could write (a customer conversation, an internal number, a dated memory). Name the gap; do not invent it.

**Author assigned a stance they have not stated**
- Mechanism: the ghostwriter's opinion, in the author's voice.
- Cost: for Vivek this is a hard rule. For any author it is a credibility risk the first time a reader asks them about it in person.
- Fix: remove, or source from the author.

**Register mismatch between speech and writing**
- Mechanism: the draft copies how the author talks, but their written register differs (Vivek: tighter on the page, uses "TL;DR" structurally, names the source up front, states both branches before his position).
- Cost: reads as a transcript, not a post.
- Fix: check the voice skill for the written-register notes before judging tone.

## Things that are not faults

- Short declarative sentences in a row, if each carries a new fact.
- A list with no intro, if the list is the point.
- A flat close, if the author closes flat.
- Repetition of a key noun, if the alternative is a pronoun that forces a re-read.

Do not flag these to look thorough. The output should change something every time it speaks.
