# Issue #18 — 26 July 2026 `[KLEO]`

## T18.1 — Named case study, minimalist verdict
**Principle:** Give away the secrets, sell the implementation. People don't buy information, they buy an experienced head that gets it right the first time.
**Best for:** service providers proving craft judgement. Name the client where you have permission; anonymised case studies invite the "this sounds made up" objection.
**Material Tier:** B — requires verified specifics, no narrative needed
**Requires:** a named client and their specific starting requirements plus the outcome achieved

```
Some [group/clients] don't need [expected element].

[Person's name] is one of them.

[Introduce person / give context / give proof].

We skipped [expected addition 1].
We skipped [expected addition 2].

[Briefly list key elements that make up your approach].

Because [person] already had what mattered:
- [Key requirement 1]
- [Key requirement 2]
- [Key requirement 3]

When those [X] are solid, you don't need to add anything else.

Most people think [common practice] means more.
More [thing]. More [thing]. More [thing].

But often the strongest move is [counterintuitive quality].

Let [element] breathe.
Let [element] do the work.
Let [element] land without competing.

A [minimal version] says "[confident statement]."
A [cluttered version] says "[insecure statement]."

Good [skill/craftspeople] knows when to stop.
Most people don't.

[Question for audience or call to action].

```

## T18.2 — Before/after, "here's what I actually changed"
**Principle:** Every product or service must deliver a transformation. Sell the transformation, not the thing.
**Best for:** a founder who improved their own metric and can show the specific moves.
**Material Tier:** B — requires verified specifics, no narrative needed
**Requires:** their own specific starting/improved metrics and the exact changes they made

```

Here's what I actually changed:

1. [Change 1].
[Brief rationale].

2. [Change 2].
[Brief rationale].

3. [Change 3].
[Brief rationale].

No [ruled-out approach].
Just [actual approach].

I do this for [clients/others] every day.
I just never did it for myself.

Things were working. So I coasted.

[Improved metric]. That's the difference.

## T18.3 — The Escalating Proof Ladder
**Principle:** Stack evidence in order of increasing scale and authority. Save the strongest proof for last, like a crescendo. Also works for stacking pain points or story beats to build tension.
**Best for:** a bold market prediction the prospect actually holds.
**Material Tier:** B — requires verified specifics, no narrative needed
**Requires:** five specific real numbers, actions, or events proving the trend/prediction

```
I couldn't possibly be any more bullish on [industry/trend] right now:

> [Example 1: include specific numbers/actions/key events]

> [Example 2: include specific numbers/actions/key events]

> [Example 3: include specific numbers/actions/key events]

> [Example 4: include specific numbers/actions/key events]

> [Example 5: include specific numbers/actions/key events]

And people are still only just waking up to how big of an opportunity this is.
```

## T18.4 — Deliberate alienation
**Principle:** Call out explicitly who the content is for and who it isn't. Make people pick a side. Writing for everyone is writing for no one. Content should repel as well as attract.
**Best for:** a founder with a clear offer and a dismissive-outsider story to tell.
**Material Tier:** A — requires a verified personal narrative
**Requires:** the exact dismissive question an old acquaintance asked and the turning point since

```
I caught up with an old [acquaintance] recently and they asked me "[Dismissive question]?"

These days I don't get offended or try to prove anything when people from my past life think [pursuit] is just some side hobby.

If they want to [choose a less desirable path], be my guest. That's less competition for us [identity/role].

Since [turning point] [time period] ago, I've [unlocked results] all thanks to my [self-deprecating description] [pursuit].

But go on about how "[dismissive label]" it is.

- [Desirable outcome #3]

## T18.5 — Comment-gated lead magnet
**Principle:** The tactic still works; what people hate is the worthless resource behind it. Hormozi's lead magnet rules apply: pick one narrow problem and solve it completely, make it good enough to have charged for, deliver a win they feel in minutes.
**Best for:** prospects with a genuine asset to give away. Skip if the resource is thin.
**Material Tier:** B — requires verified specifics, no narrative needed
**Requires:** specific dollar amounts spent, resulting income/metrics, and a real resource to give away
**Caution:** overused in some niches. Check the prospect's feed before recommending it.

```
The [tool/asset] behind my [project] cost me [small dollar amount]. [Brief anecdote about what you did to acquire it/start].

[Self-deprecating detail about not knowing something a novice wouldn't know].

That [tool/asset] is one piece of a setup that cost me [total dollar amount] in total, and the [project] it built has made me [large result]. We're at [current metric] now!

[Visual reference: image showing the actual setup or workspace behind the project]

I created [explain resource you put together].

A few honest lessons in there too:

1. [Principle/lesson/mistake 1]. [Briefly elaborate].

2. [Principle/lesson/mistake 2]. [Briefly elaborate].

3. [Principle/lesson/mistake 3]. [Briefly elaborate].

Want the full [resource] plus my [complete list/related asset]?

1. Like this post
2. Comment "[keyword]"

And connect with me if we're not already, so I can DM it to you.

### Issue #18 micro-playbooks
- **Constraints beat range.** 4 signature phrases (Katelyn Bourgoin's "ownable ideas"), 3 content categories, 2 formats, 1 platform. Repeat an idea enough and people associate it with you.
- **Engage before expecting engagement.** Support costs seconds and doesn't need to be eloquent.
- **It's never too late.** Mark Manson's grandmother-at-62 piano story. Useful raw material for a starting-late post.

### Issue #18 creator insights
- Nausheen I. Chen: people form an image of you regardless, so control it. Want to be seen as a speaker? Post stage photos.
- Niall Ratcliffe: copy is a multiplier, but the idea sets the ceiling. His team spends a full day on ideas alone.
- Daniel Bustamante: treats LinkedIn analytics as market research. A 1.5x outlier post is enough signal to expand into a lead magnet or newsletter.
- John Hamilton: Featured section stacked with proof answers "is this real?" Generates 30–40 leads a month.
- Morgan J Ingram: 10,000+ video DMs, ~35% response rate. The Movie Trailer Method — 10 secs why you're reaching out, 30 secs the value, 10 secs a collaborative CTA. **Directly relevant to Adi's outreach loop.**
- Jay Clouse: imitate before you innovate. Build a POV by noticing when others' ideas feel off.

## T17.4 — A vs B comparison
**Principle:** Comparison posts invite disagreement, which drives comments, and imply a verdict, which aids decisions. Lead with the verdict in the hook rather than withholding it — that provokes the "why?" and creates curiosity.
**Best for:** a prospect with a real tool, vendor, or method switch to report. Very strong in technical or trade niches where buyers compare constantly.
**Material Tier:** B — requires verified specifics, no narrative needed
**Requires:** two specific tools/methods compared with real prices, observations, and a stated verdict

```
I [made surprising product choice - e.g. downgrade].

Here's what happened:

[time period] ago I was [outline relatable problem you had with tool(s)].

I experienced [negative emotion/state].

So I ran an actual test to put some data behind my [instinct/hunch].

I [outline solution/test you implemented] to both [tool/method A] and [tool/method B]:

[Option A] ([price]):
- [Observation 1]
- [Observation 2]
- [Observation 3]

[Option B] ([price]):
- [Observation 1]
- [Observation 2]
- [Observation 3]

Before you say "[anticipated objection]" No. [Your counterpoint in 1-2 lines].

Anyway, the winner was [give your verdict].

It's been [time period] now and [outcome/result]. I'm still [current status].

Other than [secondary benefit], the biggest thing I learned is that [reframe the real lesson].

[Punchy closing line that restates the lesson memorably].
```

## T17.5 — Micro-moment, macro-lesson
**Principle:** A 14-word exchange can carry a full lesson. Notice small moments, reflect, and put yourself into the content.
**Best for:** the highest-ceiling template here and the hardest to fake. Only use where research surfaced a real anecdote. Do not invent the moment.
**Material Tier:** A — requires a verified personal narrative
**Requires:** a real quoted exchange/moment that cannot be invented, plus their actual reply

```
I [describe scene where someone says or does something that triggers a reaction in you]:

"[Quote the exact phrase that triggered it]"

[Your initial thought/reaction].

"[Quote your reply]."

[Break down the significance of that brief interaction].

[State what was wrong/right about it and why you reacted the way you did].

[Relate prior scenario to a niche-specific scenario].

[Give an illustrative example].

[Reveal the hidden question or meaning sitting underneath those examples]

There's another way.

[State your core philosophy or solution in one line]

[Give some practical advice/tactics].

Imagine [person in your opening scene] said:

"[The alternate, low-pressure version of the triggering line]"

I would've [positive reaction].
"[Your honest, unguarded response in that alternate version]"

[Briefly summarise why this interaction is better].

[Name the concept or framework behind this technique]

[Key takeaway(s)].
```

### Issue #17 micro-playbooks
- **Write with conviction.** Strong opinions, loosely held. Cut hedging words. "Your network decides every opportunity you will ever get" beats "it might help to build your network." Guardrail: conviction without breaking believability.
- **People buy outcomes, not products.** Nobody is excited about a plane ticket; they're excited about the Maldives. Frame everything as pain state → dream state.
- **Consistency fallbacks.** Repurpose a past winner, lean on a repeatable format, turn best-performing posts into reusable templates.

### Issue #17 creator insights
- Jake Ward: create what you would consume.
- Courtney Johnson: post before email, Slack, or client work. 8–9am, protected.
- Lara Acosta: specific enough to show authority, generic enough to reach. Broad hook, specific body.
- Paolo Trivellato: 40 comments books 4 qualified calls. Low engagement, high conversion, because of stacked case studies everywhere. "Be your own case study."
- Cam Trew: people underrate what they know until they sit down to write it.
- Jasmin Alić: expertise is opinions, not information.

## T16.2 — Apply this "everyone wants X, nobody wants Y" thesis hook to almost any domain
**Principle:** People want the big outcome but resist paying the real price for it; naming a specific authority/data source instead of vague stats lends the contrarian thesis instant credibility.
**Best for:** analysts and educators who can cite named studies or data to challenge a comfortable assumption their audience holds about success.
**Material Tier:** B — requires verified specifics, no narrative needed
**Requires:** a named study/authority and its specific data findings that contradict an assumption

```
Everyone wants [desirable outcome].
Nobody wants to do what it actually takes.
And I have bad news, because it takes...a LOT.

[Authority/source] finally confirmed that [common assumption] is [wrong/misguided]. [Briefly explain why].

So if [precondition], you likely won't [desired result].

Then there's the question of [related unresolved issue].

[Data source] data shows [surprising finding that contradicts assumption]. Not [expected answer]. [One-line explanation of why].

The logic:

[Core mechanism, stated plainly, first cause].

[Second cause explained]. [Include second data point - if applicable].

You heard that correctly: [punchy restatement of the insight].

And finally, dear [target audience], [common goal] might not be the [desirable thing] you think it is. Another study by [source] found [briefly explain surprising finding].

The rest [alternative reason for the outcome], not because [original assumption].

So stop chasing [false goal].
Build [the real, sustainable goal].

[Image that matches the post]

### Issue #16 micro-playbooks
