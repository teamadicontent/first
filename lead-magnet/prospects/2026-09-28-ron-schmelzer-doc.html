Post 1: The hiring math

Format: Contrarian
Why this one: Your Hiring Without Hiring numbers are some of the sharpest stuff you've published. They're buried in the newsletter. This puts them where founders scroll.

The difference between hiring for a task and automating it:

 

One of them still costs you $6,250 a month when revenue drops.

 

A $60K hire runs about $75K a year once you add benefits, taxes and overhead. It takes 2 to 4 months to go from job post to a productive person. Then come the meetings, the syncing, the managing.

 

Automating the same task? Around $100 a month. Often built in 2 days. No benefits. No onboarding.

 

For a microteam, the first teammates should be AI agents, SOPs and automations. Hire people for the work only people can do.

 

So before your next job post, write down the task. Then ask if a system could do it by Friday.

How big does a team need to be to build something huge?

 

I've collected 23 microteam success stories for the podcast so far.

 

The usual assumption: growth means headcount. More revenue, more people, more managers.

 

The good news? Some of the biggest outcomes came from tiny teams.

 

Here are 5:

 

1. WhatsApp

900M users with around 50 engineers. They said no to feature bloat.

42 people. $37.6M in revenue per employee.

About 50 employees. Ugly on purpose. Still printing money.

Built by 1 person. Among Us took 3. Doom took 4.

Post 6: The 5 Laws of Scalemaxxing

Format: Named framework
Why this one: Scalemaxxing is a great, ownable name. A native post that walks through all 5 laws is the kind people save and share.

How to scale without scaling headcount (even if you're a team of 3).

 

I've had three exits. These days I spend my time studying the microteams outscaling companies many times their size.

 

Here's the framework I teach.

 

I call it the 5 Laws of Scalemaxxing.

 

* Leverage over labor

Before you hire, ask what tech could do it.

 

* Repeatable processes over custom chaos

If you do it twice, write it down. Do it a third time, build the system.

 

* Master time

Time is the rarest resource a small team has. Treat it like currency.

 

Now you've got systems and time back. How do you keep moving?

 

* Celebrate momentum

Stack the micro wins until they turn into macro momentum.

 

* Harness community

Don't hire ten marketers. Mobilize a thousand believers.

 

Try one law a week for five weeks and see what changes.

 

Small team. Big scale.

 

Which law is your team worst at?

 

#Scalemaxxing #SmallTeamBigScale

Post 7: Your website builder wants to be your COO

Format: Prediction
Why this one: Your Forbes pieces usually go out as link posts. This turns last week's column into a take your microteam audience can argue with.

I'm interested to see if the next 10-person company never hires an ops team.

 

It just rents one from its website builder.

 

Here's what I'm noticing:

 

* Wix launched Symphony to coordinate AI agents across business tasks

* Hostinger and GoDaddy are rolling out bigger AI business assistants

* The pitch moved from "build your site" to "run your business"

 

From my own Forbes reporting this year:

 

* AI is moving from gold rush to utility

* Enterprise returns are still modest and adoption is uneven

 

A big part of scaling a microteam next year will come from:

 

* Picking one platform to run the back office

* Writing down your processes so agents can actually follow them

* Keeping humans on the work customers can feel

 

Website builders want to be your COO. Microteams should let them. Carefully.

 

Would you let your website platform run your operations?
