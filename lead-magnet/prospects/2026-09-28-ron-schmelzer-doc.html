Post 1: The hiring math

Format: Contrarian
Why this one: Your Hiring Without Hiring numbers are some of the sharpest stuff you've published. They're buried in the newsletter. This puts them where founders scroll.

A $60K hire costs you $6,250 a month. Even in the months revenue drops.

 

That's the all-in number. About $75K a year once you add benefits, taxes and overhead. And it takes 2 to 4 months to go from job post to a productive person. Then come the meetings and the managing.

 

Automating the same task costs around $100 a month. It can often be built in 2 days, and there's nobody to onboard.

 

For a microteam, the first teammates should be AI agents, SOPs and automations.

 

So before your next job post, write down the task. Then ask if a system could do it by Friday.

 

#SmallTeamBigScale #Scalemaxxing

Post 6: The 5 Laws of Scalemaxxing

Format: Named framework
Why this one: Scalemaxxing is a great, ownable name. A native post that walks through all 5 laws is the kind people save and share.

You can scale without scaling headcount, even as a team of 3.

 

I've had three exits. These days I study the microteams outscaling companies many times their size.

 

The framework I teach is the 5 Laws of Scalemaxxing.

 

* Leverage over labor

Before you hire, ask what tech could do the job.

 

* Repeatable processes over custom chaos

Your first teammates are SOPs and automations.

 

* Master time

Time is the rarest resource a small team has. Treat it like currency.

 

* Celebrate momentum

Stack the micro wins until they add up to macro momentum.

 

* Harness community

Don't hire ten marketers. Mobilize a thousand believers.

 

Try one law a week for five weeks and see what changes.

 

Which law is your team worst at?

 

#Scalemaxxing #SmallTeamBigScale

Post 7: Your website builder wants to be your COO

Format: Prediction
Why this one: Your Forbes pieces usually go out as link posts. This turns last week's column into a take your microteam audience can argue with.

I'm interested to see if the next 10-person company never hires an ops team, and rents one from its website builder instead.

 

Wix, Hostinger and GoDaddy are already moving that way:

 

* Wix launched Symphony to coordinate AI agents across business tasks

* Hostinger and GoDaddy are rolling out bigger AI business assistants

 

The pitch has moved from "build your site" to "run your business."

 

It fits what I've been reporting in Forbes this year. AI is moving from gold rush to utility.

 

For a microteam, that makes two things matter more next year:

 

* SOPs written clearly enough for an agent to follow

* Automating a task before hiring for it

 

Would you let your website platform run your operations?
