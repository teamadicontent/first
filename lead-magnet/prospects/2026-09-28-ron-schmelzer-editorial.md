## Post 1: The hiring math
- Fixes
  - "That's the all-in number."
    - Fault: pre-announces the breakdown that follows.
    - Cost: a line that exists only to reach the next line.
    - Fix: cut; start the paragraph at "About $75K a year once you add benefits, taxes and overhead."
  - "Then ask if a system could do it by Friday."
    - Fault: the "by Friday" deadline is a prescription Ron hasn't made; his source says "2 days".
    - Cost: small, but it's a stance under his name.
    - Fix: "Then ask if a system could do it in 2 days."
- Loops: opened "$6,250 a month" (L1), paid L3. Sequential, correct.
- Hold map: holds L1 to the $100 line; loses slightly at "That's the all-in number."
- Substitution: any automation consultant could post these numbers. Fix: "I ran these numbers in my Hiring Without Hiring issue." (true, it's his newsletter).

## Post 3: 5 tiny teams, huge outcomes
- Fixes
  - "Most founders still assume growth means headcount. More revenue, more people."
    - Fault: claim-shaped sentence with no specific, set against an imagined crowd.
    - Cost: generic opener; any post about lean teams starts here.
    - Fix: cut; go from "I've collected 23 microteam success stories for the podcast so far." straight to "Here are 5:"
  - "900M users with around 50 engineers."
    - Fault: stale figure (around 2015) written in present tense.
    - Cost: a reader who knows WhatsApp's current size will correct him.
    - Fix: "Reached 900M users with around 50 engineers."
  - List items (WhatsApp / OnlyFans / Craigslist / Pieter Levels / Minecraft)
    - Fault: grammar drift. Five items, five sentence shapes, and item 5 crams in three examples.
    - Cost: the spine loses rhythm on its last beat.
    - Fix: one shape per item, "[team size]. [outcome]." e.g. "5. Minecraft / 1 person. One of the best-selling games ever made." Only if Ron confirms that claim; otherwise keep "Built by 1 person." and drop Among Us and Doom.
  - "Hiring is the most common way to grow, and these teams skipped most of it."
    - Fault: restates the list the reader just read.
    - Cost: spends the payoff twice.
    - Fix: cut.
- Loops: one framework loop ("23 stories... here are 5"), paid. Sequential.
- Hold map: holds on the list; loses on the line after it.
- Substitution: passes because of "23 stories for the podcast" and the P.S.

## Post 5: Three waves
- Fixes
  - "Before all this: MIT, where my advisor was Rodney Brooks."
    - Fault: "all this" points at nothing the reader has seen yet.
    - Cost: the first line makes the reader re-read.
    - Fix: open on "2000: Started ZapThink, an analyst firm covering XML, web services and SOA." Move MIT to the second line: "Before that: MIT, where my advisor was Rodney Brooks."
  - The timeline as a whole
    - Fault: no turn. It's a résumé in dated lines, with no moment, doubt or decision.
    - Cost: nothing for the reader to feel; holds only people who already know him.
    - Fix: needs one real detail only Ron has (why he sold ZapThink, or why he left PMI for Scalebrate). Name the gap; don't invent it.
  - Missing third exit
    - Fault: bio says Triple-Exit Founder; the post shows two.
    - Cost: attentive readers notice the gap.
    - Fix: ask Ron for the third, or keep the post as is and don't mention a count.
- Loops: none opened. It needs one, e.g. a real decision point.
- Hold map: holds on 2011 and 2024 (the exits); loses on "Now:".
- Substitution: passes on facts (only he has this path), fails on feel. Weakest post of the seven.
